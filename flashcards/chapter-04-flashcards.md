## Chapter 4: Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Three core categories of inappropriate Claude use? | 1) Regulated decisions without human oversight (hiring, lending, medical, legal) 2) Deceptive content (undisclosed AI-generated fake reviews/testimonials) 3) Unauthorized data sharing (pasting PII/confidential data without approval). Exam tip: scenario questions test which category a use case falls into. |
| 2 | Four data sensitivity classification tiers (lowest to highest)? | Public → Internal → Confidential → Restricted/PII. Exam tip: Restricted/PII always requires the strictest handling (redaction/anonymization) before any AI prompt use. |
| 3 | What distinguishes 'Internal' from 'Confidential' data classification? | Internal = available broadly to employees, low external risk if leaked. Confidential = restricted to specific roles/teams, material harm if disclosed (e.g., unreleased financials). Exam tip: access scope + business impact determines the tier. |
| 4 | What is the correct anonymization approach before pasting customer data into a prompt? | Replace direct identifiers (name, email, account number, SSN) with placeholders while preserving task-relevant content. Exam tip: redaction ≠ deletion — keep the data useful, remove the identifying parts. |
| 5 | Why are organizational AI policies often stricter than platform baseline safeguards? | Organizations face additional regulatory (HIPAA, GDPR, finance) and contractual obligations the general platform can't anticipate. Exam tip: always apply the STRICTER of platform policy vs. org policy. |
| 6 | Three pillars of basic responsible-AI ethics tested in CCAO-F? | Transparency (disclose AI involvement), Fairness (avoid biased/discriminatory outcomes), Avoiding deception (no presenting AI output as human-authored without disclosure). |
| 7 | What makes a use case fall into the 'regulated decision' inappropriate-use category? | The AI output is used as the SOLE basis for a high-stakes decision (hiring, credit, medical, legal) without required human review. Exam tip: human-in-the-loop review is what shifts a use case from inappropriate to appropriate. |
| 8 | What is the correct escalation path for a gray-area AI use case? | Escalate to the designated AI governance contact/committee BEFORE proceeding — do not self-decide or proceed by default. Exam tip: 'not explicitly forbidden' is a commonly missed wrong answer; ambiguity requires escalation, not assumption. |
| 9 | Risk of uploading unredacted customer/employee personal data to an AI tool without authorization? | Violates data-sensitivity/privacy rules, may breach regulatory obligations (GDPR, privacy law), and exposes the organization to compliance/legal liability. Exam tip: authorization + redaction are both required, not either/or. |
| 10 | Example of 'deceptive content' inappropriate use? | Publishing AI-generated testimonials/reviews as if written by real customers without disclosure. Exam tip: the deception is about AUTHENTICITY/DISCLOSURE, not about data sensitivity. |
| 11 | Is drafting marketing copy for human review an appropriate use case? | Yes — low-risk, assistive use with human review before publication is the model of appropriate use. Exam tip: contrast with unreviewed high-stakes autonomous decisions, which are inappropriate. |
| 12 | Should an employee ask Claude itself whether a use case complies with company policy? | No — Claude lacks visibility into and authority over internal organizational policy. Compliance determinations must go through the designated governance contact/committee. |
