## Chapter 3: Workflow Integration and Solution Design — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Three-way classification for workflow steps when integrating Claude? | AI-suitable (fully automatable), Human-required (no AI), Hybrid (AI drafts/assists, human reviews/approves). Exam tip: most real-world steps end up Hybrid. |
| 2 | Exploratory research vs. Final-answer research — key difference? | Exploratory = Claude generates options/directions for a human to validate further. Final-answer = treated as verified truth with no further checking. Exam tip: Claude output should rarely be used as a final answer without human validation. |
| 3 | Why is treating Claude's research output as a final answer risky? | There's no built-in fact-verification step, so errors can propagate into decisions presented as validated. Exam trap: assuming 'fast answer' equals 'verified answer.' |
| 4 | Classic hybrid workflow pattern for content creation | Claude drafts → human reviews/edits → human approves/publishes. Exam tip: the human retains final accountability, not Claude. |
| 5 | Red flag phrases when stakeholders frame Claude's value | Claims like 'Claude will replace the team' or 'Claude never makes mistakes' overstate capability. Correct framing pairs acceleration with required human validation. |
| 6 | What does 'process mapping' mean in workflow integration? | Breaking a workflow into discrete, sequential steps so each can be individually classified as AI-suitable, human-required, or hybrid. |
| 7 | What trade-off should be measured after integrating Claude into a workflow? | Time saved (speed of drafts/first passes) vs. quality impact (increased review time, accuracy issues). Exam tip: a time win paired with a quality loss is not an unqualified success. |
| 8 | Why avoid giving Claude full ownership of customer-facing financial or legal content? | Compliance and accuracy risk require a human-required review step — classify as Hybrid at minimum, never fully AI-suitable. |
| 9 | Good process-optimization use case for Claude | Reducing repetitive manual steps — e.g., drafting repetitive responses, summarizing recurring reports — while a human still reviews/sends the final output. |
| 10 | Setting realistic expectations with stakeholders — what should always be stated? | Claude's specific limits in the workflow (e.g., it cannot independently verify facts or be held accountable) alongside its stated value. |
| 11 | Common mistake when a team measures only 'time saved' after Claude integration | Ignoring quality trade-offs (e.g., increased review time, error correction) gives an incomplete picture of ROI. |
| 12 | Is fully excluding Claude from sensitive content ever the right classification approach? | No — blanket exclusion wastes an optimization opportunity. The correct approach is Hybrid with mandatory human validation, not full exclusion. |
